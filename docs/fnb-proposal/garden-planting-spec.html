High-demand summer crops | #1 most requested at food banks
50-80 lbs/season

🧄Garlic (Oct planting)After clearing summer crops
After Sept harvest, clear bed and plant garlic cloves in October for next year's harvest

Companion Planting

  • Basil repels thrips and whiteflies; enhances tomato flavor (Iowa State research)
  • Marigolds (Tagetes patula) target root-knot nematodes
  • Sweet Alyssum attracts syrphid flies whose larvae eat aphids
  • NEVER plant potatoes in this bed (shared blight pathogens)

Harvest Notes

  • Medium Window Tomatoes hold 5-7 days on vine
  • Long Window Peppers hold 1-2 weeks on plant
  • Determinate = concentrated harvest, no pruning needed, cage only
  • Each tomato plant produces 8-15 lbs; each pepper plant 3-6 lbs
Bed 3
The Underground Vault
Root crops and alliums | Maximum storage life, no refrigeration needed
80-120 lbs/season
🧅160 Onion Sets (16/sq ft)Dry bulb, from sets | Stores 6-9 months, NO fridge
🥕CarrotsBolero or Napoli | 16/sq ft | ~150 plants
🟣BeetsDetroit Dark Red | 9/sq ft | ~72 plants
4 corners: Chives (perennial, pollinator) Aug/Sep: Fall spinach, radishes, and arugula in cleared space

Companion Planting

  • Onions interplanted with carrots: onion scent masks carrots from carrot rust fly (UMN Extension)
  • Chives at corners provide early pollinator food and deter aphids
  • NEVER plant beans or peas near this bed (alliums suppress nitrogen-fixing bacteria)

Harvest & Storage Notes

  • Long Window Carrots stay in ground for months under mulch
  • Onions: 6-9 months storage | Carrots: 5-6 months | Beets: 4-10 months
  • Cure onions 2-3 weeks before donating; remove carrot tops to extend shelf life
  • All three crops: no refrigeration needed for food bank storage
Bed 4
Calorie Central
Maximum caloric output per sq ft | Potatoes, then fall kale + garlic
20-48 lbs potatoes + 15-25 lbs kale
Spring/Summer (April-July)
🥔 Potatoes: Yukon Gold or Kennebec 24-32 seed pieces | 1/sq ft | Deep planted 5-6 inches (NO hilling needed) 700 cal/sq ft -- highest caloric yield of any garden crop
Late July: harvest potatoes, then split bed in half for fall crops
Fall/Winter (August-December+)
🥬Fall KaleWinterbor | 8 plants, 1/sq ft | Harvest through December under row cover
🧄Overwintering Garlic~80 cloves, hardneck | Plant Oct, harvest next July | Zero care between

Key Techniques

  • Deep planting (5-6 inches) eliminates hilling, the most labor-intensive part of potato growing
  • Potatoes store 2-9 months in cool, dark conditions, no fridge needed
  • Same-season relay: early potatoes followed by fall kale and overwintering garlic
  • Cure potatoes several days in dark before donating; do not donate green or nicked tubers

Harvest Notes

  • Long Window Potatoes stay in ground weeks after vine death
  • Long Window Garlic: zero maintenance, harvest when leaves yellow
  • Do NOT rotate potatoes into Bed 2 next year (shared disease with tomatoes)
  • Garlic stores 6-8 months at room temp; the ultimate food bank crop
Bed 5
Storage and Protein Workhorse
Butternut squash + bush beans + pollinator herbs | Place nearest apple trees
20-30 lbs squash + 15-25 lbs beans
🎃Trellis: 2 Butternut Squash (Waltham)Trained vertically on north side | C. moschata resists squash vine borer
🫘Bush BeansProvider or Contender | 9/sq ft | 3 succession sowings (May, June, July)
🌿DillParasitic wasps
🌿CilantroResow every 3 wk
🌿OreganoPerennial pollinator
🌼NasturtiumsTrap crop for squash bugs

Companion Planting

  • Nasturtiums: research-backed trap crop for squash bugs (SARE GS17-171)
  • Cilantro and Dill umbel flowers attract parasitic wasps and hoverflies across all beds
  • Oregano is a perennial pollinator magnet and culinary staple
  • Position this bed closest to apple trees for squash pollination

Harvest Notes

  • Long Window Butternut stays on vine until rind hardens
  • Short Window Bush beans: 2-3 day window, pods toughen quickly
  • Butternut stores 3-6 months at room temp, NO fridge
  • Cure squash at 80F for 1-2 weeks before donating
  • Bundle and label herbs for donation (recipients often unfamiliar with fresh herbs)
Harvest Window Guide

How long crops stay harvestable on the plant

With only 2-3 volunteers visiting a few times per week, harvest window tolerance is critical. Crops are color-coded throughout this document:

LONG WINDOW (weeks to months)
Kale, chard, collards, carrots, beets, onions, garlic, potatoes, butternut squash, peppers, parsley, basil, oregano
MEDIUM WINDOW (1-2 weeks)
Tomatoes (5-7 days on vine), lettuce (1-2 weeks before bolting), spinach (1-2 weeks in cool weather)
SHORT WINDOW (2-4 days)
Bush beans (pods toughen in 2-3 days), cilantro (bolts fast in heat). Requires near-daily monitoring at peak.

Pollinator strategy: The existing apple trees on the lot attract bees in April-May that will continue visiting squash flowers in summer. Bed 5 is positioned nearest to these trees. Herb and flower plantings across all beds (basil, cilantro, dill, oregano, parsley, chives, nasturtiums, marigolds, sweet alyssum) create continuous bloom from April through October, providing biological pest control and boosting yields on all pollinator-dependent crops.
