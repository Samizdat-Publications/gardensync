Sec.02

Planning a bed

Companion-aware

01  /  Companion planting

Friends drawn, quarrels flagged

Every plant carries its companions and its antagonists. Place one and the threads draw themselves — dashed green between plants that help each other, red where two crops will fight.

Here tomatoes sit with basil and marigold, and carrots with onions: the classic pairings, made visible rather than remembered.

02  /  The plant library

77 plants, searchable by what you need

Search by name, or by what you're after — low water, easy, companion to tomato. Filter by type, sort by what's in season now.

  • Spacing, days to harvest, water and sun at a glance
  • Seed-starting dates calculated from Canton's frost dates
  • Care notes for the whole season
  • Add your own seeds, including OCR from a photo of the packet

03  /  Containers

Seven kinds of ground

Not everything is a raised bed. Round pots, planters, grow bags, window boxes, potato towers and in-ground mounds each have their own footprint and their own rules about what will be happy in them.

Mix them freely — the square-foot maths follows the vessel.

04  /  Reading the garden

What the plan adds up to

A stats strip totals the garden the moment anything changes: plants, varieties, beds in use, average coverage, how many companion pairings you've managed, how many conflicts remain, water demand, and days to first harvest.

Today's Tasks sits above it all — what to sow, transplant and pick this week, with the season's progress bar running from first sowing to last frost.

Sec.03

Through the season

Zone 6a

05  /  Grow schedule

The whole year as a Gantt chart

Built from what you actually planted, not a generic calendar. Colour-coded for start-indoors, transplant, direct sow and harvest, with every date calibrated to Canton's last frost of April 18 and first frost of October 28.

06  /  Harvest log

Count what actually feeds people

Record every pick in pounds, per bed, with a flag for what went out to the community. Set a season goal and watch the bar fill.

  • Top producer, best bed, average days between picks
  • Donation rate — the number that matters for mutual aid
  • Full harvest history, exportable with the garden

07  /  Volunteers

A crew, not a staff roster

Assign beds to people with availability levels, and let the weekly task list generate itself — water checks and weed patrol, per bed, per person.

Built for spotty availability, because mutual aid runs on real life rather than corporate schedules.

08  /  Climate

Live weather, local frost

Current conditions and a 7-day forecast from Open-Meteo — no key required — with a status readout telling you whether it's an ideal growing temperature and how many days remain until first frost.

Monthly rainfall and growing-season temperature curves are drawn on canvas from Canton's climate record.

Sec.04

Two assistants

One needs no key

09  /  Garden Buddy

Ask it to do the planting

The planner's assistant runs on Claude Sonnet 5 with nine tools — it can place plants, clear and organise beds, rename them, apply a template, and answer scheduling questions against your actual garden.

"Plant tomatoes in Main Veggie Bed."   "Set up a Three Sisters garden."   "What should I plant this time of year?"

It calls the API straight from your browser with a key you supply — nothing passes through a server of ours.

10  /  Ask the almanac

Answers with no key at all

The almanac's companion reads the plant library and the garden in front of it directly. Sowing calendars, companions and quarrels, container suitability, spacing, what's ripe on the date you're viewing, the season's projected yield, what wants doing this week.

No account, no key, no network — answers land in about 3ms. Where a key is configured, the fuller model takes over instead.

Sec.05

A Quiet Almanac

The second edition

11  /  The season ribbon

Drag the pin, watch the year turn

The whole year sits at the top of the page. Move the pin to any date and the garden re-renders as the almanac imagines it then — the same beds, a different month.

  • Before sowing — dashed ghosts, waiting
  • Growing — small sprouts, filling out
  • Ready — a gold ring around the plant
  • Spent — faded to sepia

12  /  Companion halos

Guidance before you commit

Pick up a seed packet and the garden answers before you plant anything. Every friend already in the ground glows moss green; every quarrel blushes terracotta.

The plant's page opens alongside, and the ticks show which of its companions and antagonists are actually in your beds — not a generic list.

13  /  A plant's page

Arcs to its neighbours

Tap anything planted and the almanac opens its page, drawing arcs across the bed to the friends and quarrels standing near it. A year strip shows its whole cycle; below sit its care notes and a box to log what you picked.

14  /  The potting shed & the printable year

For the crew, on paper

Add raised beds in three sizes, or six kinds of container — each drawn with its own chrome, each honest about what will sulk in it.

Then print the year: every sowing, transplant, succession round and first harvest, month by month, for people who don't want a screen in the garden.

15  /  Learnable in a minute

Five paragraphs, no tour

The whole interaction model fits on one card: pick a packet and tap a square; drag the pin to travel the year; tap a plant to meet it; click a bed's name to rename it; everything autosaves.

The garden is also fully navigable from the keyboard — each plot is one tab stop, arrows walk its plants in reading order, Home and End jump to the ends.

Sec.08

Under the hood

No build step
No tooling
No bundler, no framework, no npm. Every module is a plain <script> tag and communicates through one shared global state object. Clone it and open the file.
Drawn, not templated
Beds, companion threads, climate curves and harvest charts are all drawn programmatically on <canvas> or inline SVG. No D3, no Chart.js.
Yours to keep
Autosaves to localStorage, with optional Supabase cloud sync. Export JSON, save a PNG, or copy a share link that folds the entire garden into the URL. Full undo/redo.
Offline-first
Everything except weather, the AI features and cloud sync works with no network at all. Nothing requires you to sign in.
AI, on your key
Garden Buddy runs on Claude Sonnet 5 with nine tools — placing plants, clearing and organising beds, answering schedule questions. It calls the API straight from your browser with a key you supply; it never touches a server of ours.
